### [!] Suspicious Behavior: Discontinuity in Path Reduction
In [itu_models.py](file:///home/satyansh/leo_meo/src/satlinksim/domain/link/itu_models.py#L42-L50), the path reduction factor reduction calculation contains a hard elevation threshold of $10^\circ$:
```python
r = np.where(elevation_deg <= 10,
             1.0 / (1.0 + 0.78 * np.sqrt(L_g * itu_k) - 0.38 * (1 - np.exp(-2 * L_g))),
             1.0)
```
This causes a **step discontinuity** at the boundary:
* At **$9^\circ$ elevation**, the path length is reduced by **44.3%** ($r = 0.5568$).
* At **$12^\circ$ elevation**, the reduction factor instantly jumps to **$1.0000$** (0% reduction).
In reality, the ITU-R P.618 formula applies a continuous, smooth reduction factor for all elevations to avoid step changes in computed attenuation as a satellite passes overhead.

### Cited Papers & Standards
* **ITU-R P.676-12**: *Attenuation by atmospheric gases.* International Telecommunication Union, Geneva.
* **Atmospheric Line Absorption**: Liebe, H. J. (1989). *MPM—An atmospheric millimeter-wave propagation model.* International Journal of Infrared and Millimeter Waves, 10(6), 631-650.

### [!] Suspicious Behavior: Static Gaseous Path Height
In [itu_models.py](file:///home/satyansh/leo_meo/src/satlinksim/domain/link/itu_models.py#L57-L65), the simulator calculates gaseous absorption by multiplying the specific attenuation by a static height factor of `10.0` km:
```python
return (gamma_oxy + gamma_wv) * 10.0 / np.sin(np.radians(np.maximum(elevation_deg, 5.0)))
```
Because the physical equivalent path height of oxygen is actually $\approx 6$ km and water vapor is $\approx 2.2$ km, using a static $10\text{ km}$ multiplier severely overestimates the attenuation, resulting in **4x higher gaseous absorption** than the real world.

## 5. Tropospheric Scintillation

### Validation Table
We validate tropospheric amplitude fluctuation standard deviation ($\sigma_{\text{scint}}$) across a frequency sweep from 10 GHz to 40 GHz.

| Frequency (GHz) | Simulator $\sigma_{\text{scint}}$ (dB) | Real-World Trend (ITU-R P.618) | Discrepancy |
| :--- | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **10.0 GHz** | 0.002800 dB | Low scintillation | Base reference |
| **20.0 GHz** | 0.002674 dB | Scintillation should increase ($\propto f^{7/12}$) | Incorrect negative scaling |
| **30.0 GHz** | 0.002547 dB | Scintillation should increase | Incorrect negative scaling |
| **40.0 GHz** | 0.002417 dB | High scintillation | Incorrect negative scaling |

### Cited Papers & Standards
* **ITU-R P.618-13 (Karasawa Model)**: Karasawa, Y., et al. (1988). *A prediction method of tropospheric scintillation on earth-space paths.* IEEE Transactions on Antennas and Propagation, 36(11), 1608-1614.
* **Empirical Scintillation Studies**: Banjo, O. P., & Vilar, E. (1986). *Measurements of fine structure of amplitude scintillation in Line-of-Sight Earth-space paths.* IEEE Transactions on Antennas and Propagation, 34(1), 58-66.

### [!] Suspicious Behavior: Scintillation Decreases with Frequency
In the simulator [itu_models.py](file:///home/satyansh/leo_meo/src/satlinksim/domain/link/itu_models.py#L67-L77), scintillation standard deviation **decreases** as frequency increases (30 GHz / 10 GHz ratio is **0.9097** instead of the ITU-expected **1.8981**).
This occurs because the formula is missing the crucial frequency scaling term ($f^{7/12}$). As a result, only the aperture integration factor $g(x)$ scales with frequency:
```python
x = 1.22 * D_eff**2 * (freq_ghz / 300.0)
```
Because $g(x)$ *decreases* as the antenna beamwidth narrows at higher frequencies (aperture smoothing), the absence of the $f^{7/12}$ positive scaling term causes the integration factor to dominate, leading to a physically incorrect negative scaling relationship with frequency.
